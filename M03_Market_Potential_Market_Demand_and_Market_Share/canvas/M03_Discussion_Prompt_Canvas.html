Background

Vantara Outdoors has been running an internal strategic debate for three months. Two members of the senior leadership team have reached opposite conclusions from the same data: a Market Share Index of 8.1%, a Market Development Index of 66.7 in the company's current narrow market versus 73.7 in a broader definition, and a Share Development Index of 35.9. Both executives are citing real numbers from Vantara's data. Neither is wrong about the numbers — they disagree about which numbers should drive the decision.

The VP of Sales's Position

Look at our numbers. Our Market Share Index is around 8%, and our actual dollar share is 8%: those are essentially the same, so the gap between what we should be capturing and what we're actually getting is negligible. Meanwhile, our MDI is 66.7 — we're already past the halfway point in our market's development, and crowding is coming. I'd rather invest now in new product development and move into adjacent outdoor recreation categories, where the market is larger and we can establish a position before others do. The share-point value in the broad market is $280 million per percentage point, versus $24 million in our current narrow market — an 11.7× difference. We should be playing for the bigger prize.

The CMO's Position

With respect, that's exactly backwards. Our MSI is 8.1%, not because the market is crowded, but because 62% of our potential customers have never heard of us. Our awareness leakage alone is larger than our entire market share. Our SDI is 35.9, which means we're achieving barely a third of our share potential in the market we already understand and serve. Broadening our market definition doesn't fix that problem, it hides it: we would go from 8% dollar share in a market we know to 0.69% share in a market we don't. And the broad market's MDI is 73.7, even more developed than ours. I want to fix the awareness gap first. Then we can talk about expansion.

Part 1: Your Position

Take a clear position. Side with either the VP of Sales or the CMO. Using specific numbers from this module — MDI, SDI, market share leakage percentages, or share-point values — defend your position in 150–200 words. The best posts will explain not just what the data says, but why the data the other side is citing is less persuasive in this situation.

Part 2: Devil's Advocate

Now make the strongest case for the position you just argued against. Use specific data from this module to support the opposing argument. What is the best evidence the other side has? Where is your own argument most vulnerable? (100–150 words)

What "Specific Data" Means

The best posts cite at least two specific numbers from this module: MDI 66.7 or 73.7, SDI 35.9, awareness leakage 62%, or the share-point values of $24M versus $280M. Saying "both sides have valid points" is not a data-supported argument. Saying "Vantara's SDI of 35.9 means it is capturing barely a third of its own share potential in a market it already understands, which the 62% awareness leakage explains directly" is a data-supported argument. The numbers matter.
