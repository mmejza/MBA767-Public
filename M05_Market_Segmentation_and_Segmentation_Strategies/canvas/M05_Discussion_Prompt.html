Background

Hearthstone Kitchenware has completed a needs-based market segmentation study. Three segments have been identified:

Segment Market Size Annual Growth # Competitors Company Revenue Share Marketing ROI
Home Chefs 16% of market 25%/yr 4 11% of company revenue 195%
Everyday Cooks 56% of market 3%/yr 24 68% of company revenue 41%
Gift Buyers 28% of market 12%/yr 9 21% of company revenue ~120%

The company has served Everyday Cooks for 18 years. Its sales force, retail relationships, and service infrastructure are all calibrated around that segment. After reviewing this data, the VP of Sales and the VP of Marketing have reached sharply different conclusions — both working from the same numbers.

The VP of Sales's Position

We built this company on Everyday Cooks. Walking away from 68% of our revenue is reckless. Yes, our Marketing ROI is lower there than in Home Chefs — but the absolute revenue is real. Abandoning loyal customers and established retail relationships to chase a smaller segment is not a strategy. It is a gamble.

The VP of Marketing's Position

We are not walking away from revenue. We are walking away from a trap. The health insurance company in Chapter 5 stayed in their lowest-value segment too long and spent years unwinding the damage. Our Marketing ROI in Everyday Cooks is 41%. In Home Chefs it is 195%. Every dollar we invest in Everyday Cooks returns less than a quarter of what the same dollar would return in Home Chefs. That is not loyalty — that is a misallocation.

Part 1: Your Position

Take a clear position. Side with either the VP of Sales or the VP of Marketing. Using specific data from the segment table — at least one attractiveness factor (growth rate, competitor count, or channel access) and at least one profitability metric (Marketing ROI, NMC, or revenue) — defend your position in 150–200 words.

Part 2: Devil's Advocate

Now make the strongest case for the position you just argued against. Use specific data from this module to support the opposing argument. What is the best evidence the other side has? Where is your own argument most vulnerable? (100–150 words)

What "Specific Data" Means

The best posts cite at least two specific numbers from the segment table above. Saying "Home Chefs looks more attractive" is not a data-supported argument. Saying "Home Chefs has a 195% Marketing ROI and only 4 competitors, versus 41% and 24 competitors in Everyday Cooks" is a data-supported argument. The numbers matter.
