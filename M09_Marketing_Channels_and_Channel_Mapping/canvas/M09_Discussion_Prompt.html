Background

NovaTech Distribution sells industrial sensing equipment through three simultaneous channels: direct sales, agent-distributor, and retailer-wholesale. The VP of Sales has brought a proposal to the leadership team: shift 15 points of volume from retailer-wholesale to direct, and increase direct-channel marketing and selling expense from $14.7M to $21.0M annually. The CFO has pushed back. Both are using module concepts to make their case.

The VP of Sales's Position

This is straightforward. Direct sales delivers our highest per-unit margin by a wide margin over retailer-wholesale — every point of volume we shift from retailer-wholesale to direct captures profit we're currently handing to intermediaries. The $6.3M increase in direct marketing and selling expense is small next to the contribution direct sales already generates. We should approve the full 15-point shift and stop leaving margin on the table.

The CFO's Position

Marketing contribution dollars aren't the same as marketing efficiency. Direct sales already carries by far our largest cost base — a sales force that individually acquires and services every account. Before we lock in a 43% increase in direct spend, I want to see what we're actually getting back per dollar invested, not just which channel shows the biggest number on the P&L. A channel can generate more profit and still be the worse place to put the next marginal dollar.

Part 1: Your Position

Take a clear position. Side with either the VP of Sales or the CFO. Using specific data and concepts from this module, defend your position in 150–200 words.

Part 2: Devil's Advocate

Now make the strongest case for the position you just argued against. Use specific data from this module to support the opposing argument. What is the best evidence the other side has? Where is your own argument most vulnerable? (100–150 words)

What "Specific Data" Means

The best posts cite at least two specific concepts from the module: pocket price by channel, Net Marketing Contribution, Marketing ROS, Marketing ROI, or the channel performance triad from Chapter 9. Saying "direct sales is more profitable" is not a data-supported argument. Saying "a channel's Marketing ROI depends on the ratio of NMC to M&S expense, so a channel with higher absolute NMC can still show a lower ROI if its expense base is proportionally larger" is a data-supported argument. The mechanism matters, not just the direction.
