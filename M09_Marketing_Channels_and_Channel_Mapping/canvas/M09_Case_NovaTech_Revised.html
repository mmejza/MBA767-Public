NovaTech Distribution

Marketing Channels and Channel Mapping

Case Overview

NovaTech Distribution manufactures industrial sensing equipment and sells through three channel configurations simultaneously: a direct sales force, an agent-distributor network, and a retailer-wholesale network. The VP of Sales proposes shifting volume from the retailer-wholesale channel to direct, raising direct-channel marketing and selling expense substantially. The CFO wants the numbers before signing off.

Your task is to evaluate this proposal with the module's own methods: the pocket price waterfall, channel Net Marketing Contribution (NMC) and Marketing ROI, and break-even market share under a channel mix shift, and to determine whether the proposal improves NovaTech's economics at its current market share.

Your quiz uses individualized data. Your own assigned channel prices, discounts, costs, and mix percentages appear directly inside the questions that need them. A classmate's numbers, and therefore a classmate's correct answers, will not match yours.

How This Case Quiz Works

This case is a graded Canvas quiz, not a written submission. Questions 1-10 test your own assigned NovaTech numbers: pocket price for all three channels and the weighted-average pocket price at the current mix, the Direct channel's NMC and Marketing ROI, and the weighted-average pocket price, total NMC, break-even market share, and margin of safety under the VP's proposed mix shift.

Questions 11-14 ask you to apply the same reasoning to an unrelated hypothetical company, Thornwood Industrial Supply, not NovaTech. These questions do not depend on your own Questions 1-10 numbers. Question 15 is a short reflection on your own NovaTech numbers' strategic implication; it is not scored automatically.

The quiz is worth 100 points: Questions 1-10 total 75 points, and Questions 11-14 total 25 points. Question 15 is worth 0 points and is reviewed directly by your instructor, not auto-graded.

Academic Integrity

Academic integrity note: Your data is individualized per student. Do not share your calculations or answers with other students; your numbers, and therefore your correct answers, are your own.
