Background

Apex Industrial has just completed a perceived value analysis of its FilterPro X7 commercial filtration system: an Overall Performance Index of 128 (28% more perceived benefit than the competitive average), a Cost of Purchase Index of 112 (customers perceive FilterPro X7 as 12% more expensive), and a resulting PCV Index of 16, placing FilterPro X7 in the superior value zone. Two executives on Apex's leadership team have reviewed this data and reached opposite conclusions about what to do next.

The Product Manager's Position

We are 28% better than competitors on benefits and only 12% worse on cost. The net is positive: our PCV Index is 16, which puts us in the superior value zone. Companies in the superior value zone earn above-average profitability. If we raise our price, we capture more of the value we are already delivering. The data supports it.

The Pricing Analyst's Position

Our Cost of Purchase Index is already 112. That means customers already think we charge more than competitors. Raising price moves us deeper into cost-disadvantage territory. Yes, our performance is strong, but customers evaluate both sides of the equation. A higher price makes our cost perception worse and could push us closer to the inferior value zone. I recommend holding price or improving our cost perception first.

Part 1: Your Position

Take a clear position. Side with either the Product Manager or the Pricing Analyst. Using specific data and concepts from this module — the PCV Index, the value map zone, economic value, or indifference price — defend your position in 150–200 words.

Part 2: Devil's Advocate

Now make the strongest case for the position you just argued against. Use specific data from this module to support the opposing argument. What is the best evidence the other side has? Where is your own argument most vulnerable? (100–150 words)

What "Specific Data" Means

The best posts cite at least two specific numbers from this module: the PCV Index (16), the Overall Performance Index (128), the Cost of Purchase Index (112), or the value map zone classification. Saying "our performance is strong" is not a data-supported argument. Saying "Apex's PCV Index of 16 places it in the superior value zone, which this module's profitability research associates with above-average returns" is a data-supported argument. The numbers matter.

A Note on the Right Answer

There isn't a hidden twist waiting in the case: the OPI, CPI, and PCV Index values used in this discussion are intentionally different from the ones the Apex Industrial case analysis will have you calculate yourself in Parts 1–3, but both scenarios place Apex in the superior value zone. What carries forward is the underlying tension — whether a superior-value position justifies a price increase given the cost-disadvantage risk — which your Part 4 recommendation will need to resolve with the case's own numbers. Your discussion grade does not depend on which executive you side with here: it depends on completing both parts within the stated word counts.
