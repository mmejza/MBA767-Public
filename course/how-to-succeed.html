How to Succeed in MBA 767

Practical advice for doing well in an analytical, semester-long course

The Main Idea

MBA 767 is manageable if you work steadily, read carefully, and complete module activities in the order presented. It becomes difficult quickly if you fall behind, because each week's module builds toward the Marketing Plan Project that closes the semester.

Most important advice:

Do not treat this as a course you can complete by watching videos, skimming slides, or starting assignments shortly before deadlines. The course rewards preparation, sequence, and steady effort.

1. Read the Textbook Before Gate 1

Gate 1 Reading Checks are designed to confirm that you have completed the assigned chapter reading — all five questions are drawn from content that does not appear anywhere in the slides or lecture video. They are not designed to be completed by guessing, skimming, or searching quickly for isolated terms.

Read the assigned chapter first. Then use the module overview and study guide to organize what you learned before attempting Gate 1.

2. Complete the Module in Sequence

Content modules M01–M11 are intentionally sequenced. Gate 1 unlocks the slide deck viewer, and opening the viewer unlocks Gate 2. Later activities are meant to be completed in order because they build on earlier work. The Marketing Plan Project has its own submission and viewing requirements; follow its Canvas instructions.

Overview  →  Reading  →  Gate 1  →  Slide Viewer  →  Gate 2  →  Lecture  →  Apps 1–3  →  Discussion  →  Case Quiz

3. Start the Case Quiz Prepared, Not Early

The Case Analysis Quiz is worth 125 of each module's roughly 195 points and allows only one attempt. It asks you to interpret information, perform calculations, and apply concepts from both the chapter and the slides — not to look up isolated facts. Complete the Apps first; they build the same calculation methods the case quiz then asks you to apply on your own, with the case's own numbers.

The Case Analysis Quiz and Gate 2 are open-book, open-note — but both are timed (Case: 45–50 minutes; Gate 2: 20–30 minutes, depending on the module), so "open-book" doesn't mean you have time to search for things you haven't already found. Have your notes, formulas, and worked examples organized and within reach before you start the clock, not while it's running. The case's numeric questions are also chained — a later question can depend on your answer to an earlier one — so budget a few minutes to double-check early calculations rather than rushing to the end and discovering you need to redo them.

4. Take the Quantitative Work Seriously

Marketing strategy is not only about ideas and opinions. This course requires you to work with metrics, calculations, evidence, and strategic implications. When numbers are involved, make sure you understand what the numbers mean and how they support your recommendation — you will need that same fluency for the Marketing Plan Project.

5. Use Discussions to Prepare for the Case

Discussions are not busywork. They are opportunities to test your thinking, compare perspectives, and prepare for deeper analysis. A thoughtful discussion contribution can help you clarify ideas before completing the case quiz.

6. Ask Questions Early

If you are confused, ask for help before the deadline is near. Emails sent shortly before a deadline may not receive a response before the assignment closes.

7. Know What Order Actually Matters

Many students in this course work full time and do most of a module's work Thursday through Sunday. That's a fine pattern — what matters isn't which days you work, it's the order you do things in.

In content modules M01–M11, Gate 1 must be passed before the slide deck viewer opens, and the viewer must be opened before Gate 2 becomes available. Whatever day your week starts, begin with the chapter reading and Gate 1.

Everything after Gate 2 is a strong recommendation, not a technical lock: do all three Apps before the discussion and the case quiz. Each App builds a calculation method the case quiz then asks you to apply on your own, and the case quiz allows only one attempt — walking in without having done the Apps first is the single most avoidable way to lose points on it.

8. Treat the Marketing Plan Project as Cumulative

During the last four weeks, you will advise Alder Home, a fictional business, through three connected parts: Situation Analysis; Marketing Strategy; and Resource Allocation and Contingency Planning. Use the frameworks from Modules 1–11 to interpret evidence, recommend decisions, allocate a budget, and revise your plan when conditions change.

Complete the self-assessment and Analysis Quiz, review the prepared feedback, and submit your reflection before using AI in a designated AI Permitted activity. Keep your original answers and reflection unchanged. Check consequential AI claims yourself and submit your own reasoning.

Each part also has a New Scenario Quiz completed without AI or another person’s assistance. Complete it before using AI in the next part. Check the dates for both modules in each part, and leave time to finish the Part 3 revised plan and New Scenario Quiz by December 9.

Successful Students Usually Do These Things

  • Obtain the textbook before the course begins.
  • Read the assigned chapter before attempting Gate 1.
  • Use the study guide instead of studying randomly.
  • Complete module items in the order shown in Canvas.
  • Complete the Apps before starting the case quiz.
  • Use calculations to support analysis rather than treating them as isolated tasks.
  • Check Canvas deadlines frequently.
  • Communicate professionally and ask questions before problems become emergencies.
Bottom line:

Read first, work in sequence, start early, and keep pace. That is the best formula for success in MBA 767.
