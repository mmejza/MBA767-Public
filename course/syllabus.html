Read This First

This is a sixteen-week semester, running August 24 through December 9. You will complete one module per week for the first eleven weeks, then a four-week Marketing Plan Project to close the term. Each module requires careful textbook reading, quantitative analysis, qualitative judgment, and applied work; the Marketing Plan Project asks you to bring all of it together into a single integrated recommendation.

Success in this course requires steady weekly progress. Falling behind by even one module compounds quickly, since the Marketing Plan Project draws directly on frameworks introduced in every module before it.

Important: The textbook is not optional. Module quizzes, discussions, cases, and applied assignments assume that you have completed the assigned textbook chapter before attempting the module work.

Course Description

MBA 767 is designed to build and reinforce the analytical skills needed to evaluate marketing strategies and tactics. The course emphasizes the use of marketing metrics, quantitative analysis, qualitative judgment, and strategic reasoning to analyze marketing opportunities and recommend appropriate courses of action — culminating in a term-long Marketing Plan Project applying that reasoning to a real company and market.

Course Learning Objectives

Successful completion of this course will help you:

  • Understand and apply metrics used to measure marketing strategy and tactical performance.
  • Conduct market, customer, competitor, and SWOT analyses as part of a situation analysis.
  • Use quantitative and qualitative information to evaluate marketing opportunities.
  • Recommend strategic marketing plans involving target markets, positioning, performance objectives, and marketing tactics.
  • Explain and defend marketing recommendations using evidence, calculations, and sound reasoning.
  • Synthesize the course's frameworks into a single, original marketing plan for a company of your choosing.

How the Course Works

The course consists of eleven content modules (M01–M11) completed one per week, followed by a four-week Marketing Plan Project. Each module is a sequence of learning activities and assessments. Students should complete module items in the order presented in Canvas.

Modules publish in Canvas on a rolling basis. Intro and Modules 1–4 are available from the first day of class; Modules 5–11 are finalized progressively over the first several weeks of the term, each one published before its scheduled week. This does not affect due dates or the pace of the course — only when each module's Canvas content becomes visible.

Typical Module Workflow

  1. Read the assigned textbook chapter and review the study guide.
  2. Pass the Gate 1 Reading Check to unlock the slide deck viewer.
  3. Work through the slide deck viewer to unlock Gate 2.
  4. Complete Gate 2, the applied reading check.
  5. Watch the lecture video and complete the three App Worksheet Quizzes.
  6. Participate in the module discussion, when assigned.
  7. Complete the Case Analysis Quiz.
  8. Submit all required work by the posted deadline.

Major Course Activities

Gate 1 Reading Checks

Each module begins with a Gate 1 Reading Check: five questions drawn exclusively from the assigned textbook chapter, testing content that does not appear in the slides or lecture video. Its purpose is to verify that you've completed the reading before moving on — not to generate points.

Gate 1 carries no point value. You need 80% or higher (4 of 5 correct) to pass, with up to three attempts, 15 minutes each, and per-answer feedback after every attempt. Passing Gate 1 unlocks the slide deck viewer.

Important: The slides do not contain the answers to Gate 1 questions. Complete the assigned chapter reading before attempting Gate 1.

Gate 2 Applied Reading Check

Gate 2 tests whether you can apply and connect ideas from both the chapter and the slide deck — not just recall definitions. It's worth 20 points, allows one attempt, and becomes available once you've opened the slide deck viewer.

Gate 2 is open-book, open-note. In Modules 1, 2, and 4 it's timed at 20 minutes; Module 3 is timed at 30 minutes, since it carries 13 items instead of the usual 8. The exact time limit for each module is stated on that module's own Gate 2 page — open materials are permitted, but working from your own notes and the textbook, not from another person, is what "open" means here.

Discussions

Discussions are used to share perspectives, reasoning, questions, and informed judgments about marketing strategy issues. Contributions should be thoughtful, professional, and grounded in course concepts. Students may be required to post before viewing classmates' replies.

Discussions must occur at a professional level. Offensive language, rudeness, personal attacks, and poor online etiquette are not acceptable. Judging ideas is acceptable; judging people is not.

Case Analysis Quizzes

Each module's case quiz asks you to apply marketing concepts, metrics, and methods to a realistic business situation, worth 125 of the module's roughly 195 points. It allows one attempt. These are individual assignments unless the instructor explicitly states otherwise — you are expected to submit your own work and be able to explain the reasoning behind it.

The Case Analysis Quiz is open-book, open-note, timed at 45 minutes in Modules 1 and 4 and 50 minutes in Modules 2 and 3, reflecting their higher item counts. The numeric portion of each case is chained — later questions reuse your own earlier answers — so budget time to double-check early calculations rather than rushing to the end and having to redo work. The exact time limit for each module is stated on that module's own Case Analysis Quiz page.

Modules 5–11: time limits and open-book status are still being finalized as each module is completed (see the Course Roadmap). Check that module's own Gate 2 and Case Analysis Quiz pages once it publishes.

The Marketing Plan Project

The final four weeks of the semester (Weeks 13–16) are a single cumulative project: you select a company and topic, complete a situation analysis, produce a full draft with peer review, then submit a final marketing plan applying the course's frameworks. The final submission is due before the last week of the term, which is reserved for wrap-up, course evaluations, and grading rather than new deliverables.

Responsibility for Learning

This course is intentionally challenging. Success requires consistent engagement with the textbook, careful completion of module activities, and thoughtful analysis of marketing situations.

Most students can succeed in the course if they:

  • Complete one module each week.
  • Read assigned textbook chapters before attempting assessments.
  • Begin assignments early rather than waiting until deadlines.
  • Ask questions when concepts are unclear.
  • Use feedback from earlier modules to improve later work, including the Marketing Plan Project.

The course rewards steady effort and preparation more than last-minute studying.

Rounding and Grade Changes

Grading is not an exact science. Any grade cutoff point places some students just below that line. Being just below a cutoff point is frustrating, but it is not a reason to request a grade change, re-grading of an assignment, or extra points. Scores will not be rounded up. The instructor will grade as fairly as possible, and students will receive the grade they earn.

Late Work and Availability Policies

Every graded item in this course has an Open date and a Due date. The due date is the closing date — availability ends when the item is due, with no separate grace period afterward. Once an item closes, it may not be reopened except under circumstances approved by the instructor.

Two items in the module sequence are also access-controlled rather than just date-controlled: the slide deck viewer does not open until you pass Gate 1, and Gate 2 does not open until you've opened the slide deck viewer. Everything after Gate 2 is date-controlled only.

Students are responsible for monitoring Canvas deadlines and completing work before it closes. Plan to finish before the due date, not at it.

Academic Integrity and Use of Assistance

Students are expected to complete their own work and follow all UNLV academic integrity policies. Course assignments may require calculations, written explanations, and strategic recommendations. Submissions should reflect the student's own understanding, reasoning, and analysis.

Unless an assignment specifically permits collaboration, all quizzes, cases, applied analyses, and written submissions are individual work. Students may not submit work copied from another student, generated by another person, or produced in a way that prevents the student from explaining and defending the submitted answers.

Open materials and sharing are different things, and only one of them is allowed. Gate 2 and the Case Analysis Quiz are open-book, open-note — your own textbook, your own notes, and course pages are fine to have open. Discussing specific questions or answers with another student, sharing your work, or working from someone else's notes is not, regardless of whether the assignment itself is open-book.

Artificial Intelligence Policy

This course is designed to be resistant to AI substitution — assignments are built so that a strong submission requires your own individualized reasoning, not just a plausible-sounding answer. The default assumption for every assignment is that AI tools are not used, unless an assignment's own instructions explicitly state otherwise.

Most assignments in this course are calculations or multiple-choice questions (Gate 1, Gate 2, the Apps, the Case Analysis Quiz) rather than written prose — Discussions are the only assignments that require a written submission. "Not using AI" means something slightly different for each:

  • Quizzes (Gate 1, Gate 2, Apps, Case Analysis Quiz): the answer format is a number or a selected option, but the expectation is that you did the calculation, interpretation, or reasoning that produced it. Having an AI tool compute the value, interpret the scenario, or determine the correct choice for you counts as AI use, even though what you submit is just a number or a letter.
  • Discussions: having AI draft, paraphrase, or generate any part of your post — including a first draft you then edit — counts as AI use.

The concern is not simply rule-following. When AI produces the reasoning behind a submission, it becomes impossible to tell whether the calculations, interpretation, and recommendations reflect your own understanding or the tool's output — and this course exists to build and verify that understanding. That ambiguity defeats the purpose of the assignment even when the final answer looks correct.

Before coursework begins, students complete the Start Here Quiz in the Course Information module — a short, three-question quiz confirming the syllabus has been reviewed, a technology check is complete, and this AI certification is attested. It is the first required item in the course — see Getting Started.
