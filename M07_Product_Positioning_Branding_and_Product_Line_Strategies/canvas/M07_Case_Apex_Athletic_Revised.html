Apex Athletic

Positioning, Brand Equity, and Product Line Strategy

Case Overview

Apex Athletic is a mid-price athletic footwear brand with 12 product lines. The company has operated for 18 years and has regional strength in the western United States with expanding national distribution.

A recent internal audit shows mixed brand strengths and liabilities, and leadership is deciding whether to launch a fighter brand while also reducing product-line breadth from Broad to Average at the Mature lifecycle stage. Your analysis must quantify brand-equity exposure, evaluate payback economics, and reconcile whether these strategic moves reinforce or conflict with each other.

Your quiz uses individualized data — your own assigned Brand Assets/Liabilities ratings, vulnerability-spike dimension, fighter-brand economics, and revenue base appear directly inside the questions that need them. Your data is individualized: a classmate's numbers, and therefore a classmate's correct answers, will not match yours.

How This Case Quiz Works

This case is a graded Canvas quiz, not a written submission. Questions 1-10 test your own assigned Apex Athletic numbers — Brand Assets Score, Brand Liabilities Score, Brand Equity, the vulnerability-spike scenario (new Liabilities Score, new Brand Equity, and the equity change), fighter-brand payback period, and the product-line ROA dollar impact on your assigned revenue base.

Questions 11-14 ask you to apply the same reasoning to an unrelated hypothetical company, Cobalt Audio — not Apex Athletic. These questions do not depend on your own Questions 1-10 numbers. Question 15 is a short reflection on your own Apex Athletic numbers' strategic implication; it is not scored automatically.

The quiz is worth 100 points: Questions 1-10 total 75 points, and Questions 11-14 total 25 points. Question 15 is worth 0 points and is reviewed directly by your instructor, not auto-graded.

Academic Integrity

Academic integrity note: Your data is individualized per student. Do not share your calculations or answers with other students — your numbers, and therefore your correct answers, are your own.
