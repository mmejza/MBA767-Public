Background

Cascade Power Tools sells a professional cordless tool line at $250 per unit. A new competitor has entered the market at a lower price point, and several contractor accounts have switched suppliers this quarter. The VP of Sales wants to cut price. The CFO says the math does not support it.

VP of Sales: Cut Price 10%

“Our price is too high. Competitors are undercutting us and we’re losing deals. Several contractor accounts have switched suppliers this quarter. If we drop price by 10%, I’m confident we can grow volume by 20% and hit our revenue target. We need to be competitive or we lose these accounts permanently.”

CFO: Hold Price

“A 10% price cut with our current contribution margin requires significant volume growth just to break even on profit; run the numbers. Our market elasticity is estimated at −0.85, which is inelastic. At that elasticity, a 10% cut generates around 8–9% volume growth, far short of the 20% the VP is projecting. We’d be destroying margin to chase revenue. We should hold and invest in closing the service quality gap instead.”

Part 1: Your Position

Take a clear position: side with either the VP of Sales (cut price 10%) or the CFO (hold price). Using breakeven analysis and price elasticity analysis, defend your position in 150–200 words. Show your calculation steps; do not just describe the result.

Part 2: Devil's Advocate

Now make the strongest case for the position you just argued against. What is the best evidence the other side has? Where does your own break-even or elasticity argument have a weakness? What conditions would have to be true for the opposing side to be right? (100–150 words)

What Strong Posts Look Like

The best posts show the arithmetic, not just the conclusion. Calculate the new contribution margin at the proposed cut price. Apply the break-even formula to find the minimum volume increase required. Compare that to the elasticity-implied volume gain. Then compare both to the VP’s 20% claim. The gap between what you need and what elasticity predicts is the core of this debate: your post should name that gap explicitly.
