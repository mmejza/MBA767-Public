Background

Northgate Outdoor Gear sells camping and hiking equipment directly to consumers online. The company has been running a national brand-awareness campaign for two quarters, but revenue growth has stalled even though awareness keeps climbing. The CFO wants to redirect a large share of this quarter's media budget into a one-quarter retailer trade promotion to generate immediate volume. The Brand Manager wants to protect the media budget and instead invest in fixing a specific weak point in the customer response funnel. Both are using this module's concepts to make their case.

The CFO's Position

We need volume now, not more awareness. I want to redirect 30% of this quarter's media budget into a retailer trade promotion. A promotion moves product immediately; a media campaign we can't yet prove is converting does not. If a short-term promotion costs us some brand equity, we can rebuild that later once cash flow stabilizes.

The Brand Manager's Position

Cutting media now ignores two things this module teaches us. First, our own funnel data shows the real problem isn't awareness: it's the stage right after it, where customers who already know us aren't translating that into a purchase decision, and no trade promotion fixes a stage further upstream in the funnel. Second, current media spending carries forward into future periods; cutting it now doesn't just lower this quarter's results, it lowers every future quarter's baseline too.

Part 1: Your Position

What "Specific Data" Means

The best posts cite at least two specific concepts from the module: the CRI stage hierarchy and which stage acts as the bottleneck, Gross Rating Points versus actual message comprehension, the carryover coefficient and how current spending compounds into future periods, or the risk of forward-buying from a trade promotion. Saying "we should protect the media budget" is not a data-supported argument. Saying "because CRI is a multiplicative chain, improving the bottleneck stage raises the whole funnel's output more than adding reach or frequency upstream of it" is a data-supported argument. The mechanism matters, not just the direction.
