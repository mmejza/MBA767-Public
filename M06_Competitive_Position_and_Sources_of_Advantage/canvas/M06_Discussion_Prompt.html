Background

Crestline Manufacturing competes in the industrial valve market — a different company from the one in this module’s Case Quiz, so the numbers below are its own. Last quarter, Competitor A cut prices by 8%, triggering a strategic debate among Crestline’s leadership team. The VP of Finance and the VP of Marketing have reached opposite conclusions about how to respond.

VP of Finance: Cut Price

Our cost position is real. A VCAI of 96.2 confirms we produce more efficiently than any competitor in this market. If we match the 8% price cut, we’ll lose margin in the short term, but our lower cost structure means we’ll outlast anyone who tries to compete with us on price. The competitor that started this probably can’t sustain the cut anyway. Look at their recent signals. We should cut and let the market shake out.

VP of Marketing: Hold Price

This is exactly the Prisoner’s Dilemma playing out in front of us. If we cut, we confirm that price is the competitive dimension in this market, and that destroys the differentiation advantage we’ve been building on product quality. An IAI of 27.9 means real profit potential worth protecting. We should hold price, invest in closing the service quality gap instead, and let Competitor A’s financial distress work in our favor.

What “Specific Data” Means

The best posts cite at least two specific values from the table above: Crestline’s VCAI, Differentiation Index, RMS, IAI, or payoff-matrix figures. Saying “cost advantage is real” is not a data-supported argument. Saying “a VCAI of 96.2 confirms Crestline has a lower variable cost structure than the market average, which means it can sustain a price cut longer than Competitor A” is a data-supported argument. The numbers matter — use the ones from the table, in your own words.
